Gemini 4 Carbon vs Hydrogen for coding

Read the full short answer

Short answer: use Hydrogen for everyday edits, tests, debugging and agent loops. It is the cheap, fast one ($1.50 in / $7.50 out per million tokens on the API, with a free tier). Save Carbon for the hard jobs: big refactors, migrations, root-cause hunts and architecture. And plan for December 1, 2026: that is when Carbon and Argon go from $2 / $10 to $4 / $20 per million tokens, which is exactly what Claude Opus 5.5 costs per token.

Checked Nov 16, 2026 against Google's Carbon and Hydrogen announcements, the Gemini API pricing page, the Antigravity blog, models docs and CLI releases, GitHub's changelog and Copilot pricing docs, and Cursor's model docs. Benchmark numbers below are Google's own claims, not our tests. Re-checked Nov 26, 2026: Antigravity now lists Carbon on paid Google AI Pro (not just Ultra) and Hydrogen on every plan, including Free. Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash leave Antigravity on Nov 30, 2026.

What changes in December (and why Copilot users should care)
  • Dec 1, 2026: Carbon and Argon double per token on the API. $2 / $10 becomes $4 / $20, and cached input goes from $0.10 to $0.20. Google calls the current price "introductory." Cursor's docs and GitHub's Copilot pricing docs both say they follow it: Carbon moves to $4 / $20 on Dec 1 in Cursor and Copilot too.
  • From Dec 1, Carbon costs the same per token as Claude Opus 5.5 ($4 in, $20 out, $0.20 cached). Claude Fable 5.2 stays at twice that ($8 / $40). So "Gemini is the cheap frontier option" is true for two more weeks at the token level. After that it comes down to how many tokens each model burns on your tasks.
  • Dec 14, 2026: Copilot drops Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash. GitHub's suggested replacements are Carbon (for Argon) and Hydrogen (for 3.8 Flash). Claude Fable 5, Claude Haiku 4.5 (except individual annual plans), Grok 4.6 and Kimi K3 go the same day.
  • What that means in Copilot credits: Copilot bills these models at provider list price. Argon users who switch to Carbon pay the same per token after Dec 1, but Google says Carbon uses more tokens than Argon, so expect each task to cost more. Gemini 3.8 Flash users moving to Hydrogen go from $0.75 / $3.75 to $1.50 / $7.50 per million tokens in Copilot's price table, double per token.
  • Hydrogen: no price change announced.

Sources: Gemini API pricing, Cursor docs: Gemini 4 Carbon, GitHub: Copilot models and pricing, GitHub changelog: model deprecations Dec 14. Copilot's footnote says Argon's promo price ends Dec 1 but does not restate Argon's new Copilot price; the Gemini API lists $4 / $20.

Which one should I pick?

Interactive

Our opinion, based on vendor docs and prices checked Nov 16, 2026.

How to use them without overpaying
  • Default to Hydrogen, escalate to Carbon. Google itself built Antigravity's Orchestrator on this idea: Carbon plans, Hydrogen edits and runs tests, and Carbon comes back when Hydrogen gets stuck. Google says that reached near-Carbon success on Terminal-Bench 4.1 with 24% less quota and 19% faster runs on average.
  • Turn thinking down for small jobs. Thinking tokens bill as output. Hydrogen's xhigh and Carbon's high are for hard problems, not renaming a variable.
  • Run big Carbon jobs before Dec 1 if you pay per token. A migration or audit you were going to do anyway costs half as much per token on the API, in Cursor and in Copilot until Nov 30.
  • Copilot teams on Argon: test Carbon before Dec 14, on a real task, and watch credit use. The replacement is pricier per task, not just different.
  • Keep the cache warm. Cached input is 95% off on Carbon and Hydrogen. Long sessions in one repo cost less than starting fresh each time.
  • Use Batch or Flex for non-urgent API work. Both are half price on the Gemini API.
  • Mind free-tier privacy. Hydrogen's free API tier is free because Google can use that data to improve its products. Use the paid tier for client or company code.

What we could not confirm
  • Whether Antigravity's subscription quotas will burn faster for Carbon or Argon after Dec 1. Google has not said; quotas are not published as token numbers.
  • Whether Carbon will come to Google AI Pro. Google has not said when or if.
  • Carbon's raw CursorBench 4.0 score. Google's post claims the #1 rank and "more than 2X lower cost per task" but gives no numbers.
  • Copilot's exact post-Dec 1 Argon price. GitHub's docs say Argon's promo ends Dec 1 without restating the new rate. Argon leaves Copilot Dec 14 anyway.
  • All benchmark figures on this page come from Google. We have not run our own tests.

FAQ

8 questions

What is the difference between Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Hydrogen?

Carbon is Google's strongest Gemini 4 model, built for hard, long, multi-step work such as large refactors, migrations and root-cause hunts. Google says it uses more tokens than Gemini 4 Argon to reason and verify. Hydrogen is the fast, cheaper model for everyday coding, debugging and agent loops, with four thinking levels (low, medium, high and xhigh). Google still calls Argon the ideal choice for most software engineering work.

How much does Gemini 4 Carbon cost?

On the Gemini API, Carbon is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through November 30, 2026, with cached input at $0.10. From December 1, 2026 it is $4 input, $20 output and $0.20 cached. Gemini 4 Argon has the same prices and the same December 1 change. Carbon is not on the API free tier.

How much does Gemini 4 Hydrogen cost?

On the Gemini API, Hydrogen is $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens on the paid tier, with cached input at $0.075. It also has a free tier, but Google's pricing page says free-tier data is used to improve its products. No price change is announced for Hydrogen.

Is Gemini 4 Carbon cheaper than Claude Opus 5.5?

Per token, only until November 30. From December 1, Carbon's API price ($4 in, $20 out, $0.20 cached) matches Claude Opus 5.5 ($4 in, $20 out, $0.20 cache read). Claude Fable 5.2 is $8 in and $40 out. Cost per task also depends on how many tokens a model uses, and Google says Carbon uses more tokens than Argon.

Where can I use Gemini 4 Carbon for coding?

In Google Antigravity on Google AI Ultra (app, CLI and IDE extensions), in GitHub Copilot on Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise, in Cursor on Premium and up (billed from prepaid credit at API rates), and through the paid Gemini API, including Antigravity CLI with a GEMINI_API_KEY. Google AI Pro does not get Carbon in Antigravity as of Nov 16, 2026.

Where can I use Gemini 4 Hydrogen?

In Antigravity on paid Google AI Pro and Ultra, in GitHub Copilot on Pro and up, in Cursor on Premium and up, and on the Gemini API (free and paid tiers). Gemini CLI is now aimed at Gemini Code Assist enterprise customers and its README says it is not tuned for Gemini 4 models.

Is GitHub Copilot removing Gemini 4 Argon?

Yes. GitHub's Nov 13 changelog says Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash, along with Claude Fable 5, Claude Haiku 4.5, Grok 4.6 and Kimi K3, will be deprecated across Copilot on December 14, 2026. The suggested replacement for Argon is Gemini 4 Carbon and for Gemini 3.8 Flash it is Gemini 4 Hydrogen.

What is Orchestrator in Antigravity?

A model option that lets Carbon (or Argon) plan and Hydrogen do the edits and tests, handing back when Hydrogen gets stuck. Google says it reached near-Carbon success on Terminal-Bench 4.1 while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster on average. Orchestrator (Carbon) is for Google AI Ultra; Google AI Pro gets Orchestrator (Argon).
