Gemini 4 Neon vs Opus 5.5 vs GPT-6.2 Astra: what one coding task costs

Short answer: Google's new Gemini 4 Neon ties Claude Opus 5.5 on Artificial Analysis' independent coding agent test (59 vs 58) at about half the cost per task ($6.86 vs $12.61) and in about half the time (30.5 vs roughly 65 minutes). If you pay per token and want top-tier agent results, Neon is now the cheapest way to get them.

Two catches. Claude Fable 5.2 at high effort scored the same 58 for $7.51, so Neon's edge over Anthropic is smaller than the Opus headline suggests. And GPT-6.2 Astra costs more per token ($8/$40 vs $3/$15) but less per task ($5.12 at max, $3.20 at xhigh), because Neon burns roughly three times the tokens. Astra scores 6 points lower, though.

Checked Nov 20, 2026 against Google's Neon announcement (Nov 18), the Gemini API pricing page, the Neon model card, Artificial Analysis' Neon write-up, Antigravity CLI vs Claude Code results and Codex results, the Antigravity blog and models docs, the GitHub changelog and Copilot models and pricing, and Cursor's Neon docs. Benchmark numbers are Artificial Analysis' or the vendors' own, not our tests.

What this means:

  • Neon vs Opus 5.5: same score within a point, 46% lower cost per task, and less than half the wall time. That part of the hype holds up.
  • Neon vs Fable 5.2 (high): same score, $6.86 vs $7.51, about the same time. If you already live in Claude Code, Fable at high is nearly as cheap.
  • Neon vs Astra: Astra is the cheaper and faster agent per task, but 6 points lower. Per-token price is the wrong number to compare here.
  • Neon is chatty. 177 turns per task vs 95 for Fable 5.2 (xhigh), and AA's general index page calls Neon at xhigh "very verbose" (283M output tokens on that suite vs a 65.4M median). Its cost advantage comes from lower token prices, not from using fewer tokens.

Neon vs Carbon after Dec 1

Gemini 4 Carbon (and Argon) go from $2/$10 to $4/$20 per million tokens on Dec 1, 2026. Neon stays at $3/$15, so from Dec 1 Neon is 25% cheaper per token than Google's flagship. Until Nov 30, Carbon is still cheaper per token.

On Artificial Analysis' coding agent test Neon already wins both ways: 59 vs 55, and $6.86 vs $9.75 a task. Google's model card says Neon is adapted from Carbon. Unless you're on Google AI Ultra and need Carbon's long-horizon depth, there's little reason to pick Carbon over Neon for coding after Dec 1. More: Gemini 4 Carbon vs Hydrogen.

Verdict

  • Paying per token for hard agent work: Neon in Antigravity CLI. Same results as Opus 5.5 at about half the cost.
  • On a Claude plan: don't switch just for this. Try Fable 5.2 at high effort, which matched Opus 5.5 at $7.51 a task.
  • On a budget: Codex with GPT-6.2 Astra at xhigh ($3.20, score 53) or Sol at xhigh ($0.90, score 50). Most daily tasks don't need a 59.
  • Cheapest way to try Neon on a subscription: Google AI Pro at $19.99 in Antigravity.
  • Copilot Pro+ users: Neon is now the cheaper pick per token of the three. Just don't expect AA's numbers to carry over exactly, since AA didn't test Copilot's agent with Neon.

What we couldn't confirm

  • How fast Neon uses Antigravity quota. Google publishes no quota numbers, for Neon or Orchestrator (Neon).
  • Cost in Copilot and Cursor. The list price is the same, but AA only measured Neon in Antigravity CLI. Other agents may use more or fewer tokens.
  • CursorBench. Google's model card lists Neon at 65.4% vs Opus 5.5 at 58.1% on CursorBench 4.1, and Cursor posted 63.7% for Neon at default effort. That's Cursor's in-house benchmark, not independently checked.
  • "#1 on the coding agent index." True when we checked, but by 0.1 to 0.4 points over Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5. Treat it as a tie.
  • Carbon's $9.75. AA's table doesn't say whether that uses Carbon's current $2/$10 or the Dec 1 $4/$20 price.

FAQ

Is Gemini 4 Neon better than Claude Opus 5.5 for coding?

On Artificial Analysis' Coding Agent Index v2.1 (checked Nov 20, 2026), Neon at xhigh in Antigravity CLI scored 59 (58.6) and Opus 5.5 at max in Claude Code scored 58 (58.2). That is a tie in practice. The real gap is cost and time: $6.86 and 30.5 minutes per task for Neon vs $12.61 and about 65 minutes for Opus 5.5, at API list prices.

How much does Gemini 4 Neon cost?

Gemini API paid tier: $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 cached input, $15 output (thinking tokens count as output), flat across its 1M-token context. No free API tier. GitHub Copilot and Cursor bill the same $3/$15 list price. Claude Opus 5.5 is $4/$20 and GPT-6.2 Astra is $8/$40.

Is GPT-6.2 Astra more expensive than Neon?

Per token, yes: $8/$40 vs $3/$15. Per task, no: Artificial Analysis measured Codex with Astra at max at $5.12 a task (score 53) and at xhigh at $3.20 (score 53), because Astra used about 3 to 4.7M tokens per task vs 13.8M for Neon. Neon scores 6 points higher, though.

Where can I use Gemini 4 Neon?

Gemini API (paid tier) and AI Studio, Google Antigravity on paid Google AI Pro ($19.99) and Ultra (it is the default model there, with a 512K context window in Antigravity), GitHub Copilot Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise, Cursor on Premium and up from prepaid credit, and the Gemini app on Pro and Ultra.

Will Neon be cheaper than Gemini 4 Carbon?

From Dec 1, 2026, yes per token: Carbon goes from $2/$10 to $4/$20 while Neon stays $3/$15. Until Nov 30, Carbon is cheaper per token. On Artificial Analysis' coding agent test Neon already cost less per task ($6.86 vs $9.75) and scored higher (59 vs 55).
