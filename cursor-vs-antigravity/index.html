Cursor vs Google Antigravity (Oct 2026)

Short answer: if you want Claude or Gemini, Antigravity is the better deal right now. Google AI Pro at $19.99/mo includes Gemini 4 Argon and Claude Opus and Sonnet 5.5 inside its quota, while Cursor needs Premium at $30/mo and then bills Claude and Gemini on top at API list price. Choose Cursor if you want its IDE with cloud agents, Bugbot code reviews and a simple card-paid team plan, or if Grok models are enough (Plus is $8). Choose Antigravity if you want the most model choice for $20, a free tier with unlimited Tab, or an agent that also runs in VS Code, JetBrains, Zed and Xcode.

Where Cursor wins

  • Cheapest paid entry. Plus is $8/mo if Grok models are enough for you.
  • Easy team plan. Teams from $30/seat on a card, with SSO, usage analytics and mixed seat tiers. Antigravity teams have to go through Google Cloud.
  • Annual discounts. Every Cursor tier has a yearly price; we found none for Google AI plans.
  • More built in. Cloud agents and automations, Bugbot code reviews and code hosting come with Premium.
  • Every frontier model, if you pay for it. Premium and up can use Claude, GPT and Gemini, billed at API list price.

Where Antigravity wins

  • Claude and Gemini in a $20 plan. Google AI Pro ($19.99) includes Claude Opus and Sonnet 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon inside its quota. In Cursor, the same models cost extra on top of $30.
  • A real free tier. Unlimited Tab and Command requests, plus a weekly agent quota on Gemini 3.8 Flash.
  • Use it in your current editor. Extensions for VS Code, Visual Studio, JetBrains, Zed and Xcode, plus a CLI and an SDK. You don't have to switch IDEs.
  • Five-hour refresh on paid plans. Pro and Ultra quotas refresh every five hours up to a weekly cap, so one heavy morning doesn't end your week.
  • Watch out: Claude 4.6 and GPT-OSS 120B leave the free tier on Nov 2, 2026, and the Claude 5.5 models aren't on the Pro free trial. Quotas aren't published as numbers, and there's no bring-your-own-key option.

What switching actually costs

The sticker prices above are only part of it. Here is what moving from Cursor to Antigravity (or back) costs in real money and time, based on each vendor's own terms and docs, checked Oct 22, 2026.

CostWhat it means in practice
The overlap monthCursor says to cancel at least 24 hours before renewal, and plan downgrades take effect at the end of the billing period; Google says "No refunds for partial billing periods, except as required by applicable law." Trying both properly means paying both for up to a month: Cursor Premium $30 + Google AI Pro $19.99 = $49.99. On a Cursor annual plan ($300 for Premium) the pages we checked don't promise a refund for leaving mid-year, so time the switch to your renewal.
Leftover Cursor creditClaude and GPT in Cursor are paid from prepaid top-up credit. Unused credit stays in your account until it's used or expires, currently 365 days after purchase, so anything left when you leave is money you only get back by coming back. Run it down first, or keep Auto Top-up off in the weeks before you switch.
Losing a legacy Cursor planIf you're still on legacy Cursor Pro at $20, it isn't sold to new customers. Cancel it and come back later, and the comparable plan is Premium at $30: +$10 a month, or $120 a year.

Gemini Enterprise covers the Antigravity App, CLI and IDE extensions but not the standalone Antigravity IDE. Cursor's dashboard-managed Team Rules don't carry over.

Rules and instructionsAn AGENTS.md file works in both, unchanged. Cursor project rules (.cursor/rules/*.mdc with alwaysApply, globs and description) need rewriting for Antigravity (.agents/rules/*.md with a trigger of always_on, model_decision, glob or manual). Antigravity silently drops a rule with missing or invalid frontmatter, ignores rule files in subfolders unless you list them in .agents/rules.json, and truncates any rule file over 24 KB.
MCP servers and extensionsCursor keeps MCP servers in .cursor/mcp.json (or ~/.cursor/mcp.json); Antigravity uses .agents/mcp_config.json (or ~/.gemini/config/mcp_config.json). The format is close, but remote servers must use serverUrl: Antigravity doesn't accept the url field Cursor uses, so copied remote entries fail until renamed. You won't lose your editor, though: Antigravity has extensions for VS Code, Visual Studio, JetBrains, Zed and Xcode.

Verdict: who should pick which

Pick Cursor if…

  • You want a polished AI IDE and Grok models cover most of your work ($8 Plus or $30 Premium).
  • You need a team plan you can buy today with a credit card.
  • You'll use cloud agents, Bugbot reviews and code hosting in one place.

Pick Google Antigravity if…

  • Claude or Gemini is your main model and you want it at a flat $19.99 instead of API prices.
  • You want to start free, or keep coding in VS Code, JetBrains, Zed or Xcode.
  • Your company already runs on Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise.

Honest caveat: both vendors keep their exact limits private, and $20 of quota buys different amounts of work depending on the model and task. FAQ

Is Google Antigravity free?

Yes, there is a $0 individual plan with unlimited Tab completions, unlimited Command requests and a basic weekly rate limit, using Gemini 3.8 Flash. Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-OSS 120B are also on the free tier until Google removes them on November 2, 2026. Cursor also has a free plan with limited agent and chat requests.

Is Antigravity cheaper than Cursor?

It depends on the models you want. Cursor's cheapest paid plan, Plus, is $8/month but runs Grok models only. Antigravity's paid tier is Google AI Pro at $19.99/month, which includes Gemini 4 Argon and Claude Opus and Sonnet 5.5 inside its quota. In Cursor, Claude and Gemini need Premium ($30/month) and are billed on top at API list price. So for Claude or Gemini users, Antigravity is usually cheaper; for Grok-only users, Cursor Plus is.

Can I use Claude in Google Antigravity?

Yes. Antigravity's model docs list Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (thinking) on Google AI Pro and Ultra, on a separate fixed rate limit from Gemini models. On Google AI Pro they need a paid subscription, not the free trial. Free users get Claude Sonnet and Opus 4.6 until November 2, 2026.

Does Antigravity work in VS Code or JetBrains?

Yes. Besides its own Antigravity App, Antigravity IDE and CLI (agy), Google offers Antigravity extensions for Visual Studio Code, Visual Studio, JetBrains IDEs, Zed and Xcode. Gemini 4 Argon is available in the App, CLI and extensions but not in the standalone Antigravity IDE.

How much do Antigravity's paid plans cost?

Antigravity's paid tiers come with Google AI subscriptions: Google AI Pro is $19.99/month and Google AI Ultra is $99.99/month (5x the Pro quota) or $199.99/month (20x). Usage beyond your quota can be paid with AI credits at Gemini Enterprise consumption prices. Organizations buy through Google Cloud: Gemini Enterprise Standard and Plus start at $30 per seat per month, and a pay-as-you-go option has no seat fee.

Which is better for teams, Cursor or Antigravity?

Cursor is simpler: Teams starts at $30 per seat on a credit card, with centralized billing, SSO and mixed seat tiers. Antigravity has no self-serve team plan; organizations use it through Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise (from $30 per seat, or pay-as-you-go), and the standalone Antigravity IDE isn't supported for enterprise deployments. Teams already on Google Cloud may prefer Antigravity.

What does it cost to switch from Cursor to Antigravity?

Plan on paying for both for up to a month (Cursor changes take effect at the end of the billing period, and Google doesn't refund partial periods): Cursor Premium $30 plus Google AI Pro $19.99 is $49.99. Unused Cursor top-up credit stays until it expires (currently 365 days after purchase), and if you leave a legacy Cursor Pro plan ($20) you can't get it back; the comparable plan is Premium at $30. Teams move to Google Cloud (Gemini Enterprise from $30 per seat). AGENTS.md works in both, but Cursor .mdc rules need new frontmatter and remote MCP servers need a serverUrl field in Antigravity.
